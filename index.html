02RESEARCH LAB

WHY STUDY THE MOON?

Craters, volcanic landforms and ancient ice preserve clues to solar-system history. Apollo crews returned about 382 kilograms of lunar rocks and soil, which scientists continue to study. Samples and observations help us investigate how rocky worlds formed and changed.

IN YOUR BASE

Surface sampling and lab work turn exploration into knowledge. Your mission studies represent the scientific purpose behind keeping a base operating.

03AIRLOCK + POWER

WEATHER FROM SPACE

The Moon’s extremely thin exosphere cannot make Earth-like rain, clouds or storms. Instead, lunar explorers face sharp temperature contrasts, incoming debris and energetic particles. Sunlit equatorial ground can exceed 121°C, while some permanently shadowed polar craters are colder than −246°C.

IN YOUR BASE

Plan EVAs and keep protective systems running. The game’s meteor and radiation events represent space hazards, not thunderstorms.

NASA SOURCE · Weather on the Moon ↗
04GREENHOUSE + LIFE SUPPORT

GROWING A LIVING BASE

NASA and University of Arizona researchers tested a prototype greenhouse concept for food production and recycling air and water. Plants use carbon dioxide and release oxygen through photosynthesis. Nutrient-enriched water circulates around roots, helping model a more self-sustaining life-support system.

IN YOUR BASE

Farming needs water, power and crew time. Producing food and recycling resources helps your station depend less on stored supplies.

NASA SOURCE · Lunar and Martian Greenhouses ↗
05FOOD + CREW WELLBEING

VEGGIE: FOOD IN ORBIT

Veggie is a plant-growing system aboard the International Space Station. It uses lighting and water-delivery methods to grow fresh crops as a supplement to packaged food. NASA’s fact sheet describes how fresh produce can add nutrients and variety, while caring for plants may also benefit astronaut wellbeing.

IN YOUR BASE

Assign a crewmate to farming, then make time for meals and rest. Food production and personal care are separate jobs in your base.

NASA SOURCE · NASA Veggie Fact Sheet (PDF) ↗
06SHELTER + MED BAY

RADIATION: MONITOR + SHELTER

NASA’s Space Radiation Analysis Group evaluates astronaut exposure using environmental data, radiation detectors, forecasts and shielding models. During elevated particle activity, recommendations can include moving to better-shielded areas, delaying a spacewalk or returning from one already underway.

IN YOUR BASE

Respond to a radiation warning by sheltering. The game’s treatment and health numbers simplify a complex risk; real protection emphasizes monitoring and reducing exposure.

NASA SOURCE · NASA Space Radiation Analysis Group ↗
07EXPLORATION

MOON FACT FILE

The Moon is Earth’s natural satellite, about 239,000 miles (385,000 kilometres) away. Its rocky surface carries impact craters, and its thin atmosphere is not breathable. Because the Moon rotates in step with its orbit, the same hemisphere faces Earth; the far side still receives sunlight.

IN YOUR BASE

Oxygen must come from station systems and suits. Explore carefully: a familiar view of Earth does not make the lunar surface Earth-like.
